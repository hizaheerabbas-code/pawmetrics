Why Weight Matters

A dog's weight is the single most important factor for calculating correct food portions. Overfeeding by just 10% can lead to obesity within months.

🏃

Activity Changes Everything

A highly active working dog may need up to 40% more calories than a sedentary dog of the same weight and breed. Activity level matters as much as size.

🌿

Age Adjustments

Puppies need more calories per kg than adults for healthy growth. Senior dogs typically need 20% fewer calories to maintain a healthy weight.

How Much Should I Feed My Dog? Complete Guide

Feeding your dog the right amount is one of the most important things you can do for their long-term health. Too little food leads to nutritional deficiencies, while too much causes obesity — which affects over 50% of dogs in the UK and USA.

The Simple Formula

The basic calculation for adult dogs is based on their Resting Energy Requirement (RER): RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75. From this base, a multiplier is applied depending on age, activity level, and whether the dog is neutered or intact.

Puppies Need More

Puppies under 4 months need about 3× their adult RER. From 4 months to 1 year, they need about 2× the adult rate. Always feed puppy-specific food which has the right calcium and phosphorus ratios for bone development.

Senior Dogs Need Less

Dogs aged 7 and over typically have slower metabolisms. Most senior dogs need around 20% fewer calories than middle-aged adults of the same weight. Senior-specific food also tends to have joint-supporting supplements like glucosamine.

Dry vs Wet Food Portions

Wet food contains about 75–80% water, so you need significantly more of it by weight to meet calorie needs. If feeding wet food, expect to give roughly 3–4× the gram amount you would with dry kibble to achieve the same calorie intake.

How Often Should I Feed My Dog?

Adult dogs (over 1 year) do well on 2 meals per day — morning and evening. Puppies under 6 months should eat 3–4 times daily. Large breed puppies especially benefit from smaller, more frequent meals to reduce the risk of bloat.
